All notes in `reference/` distill the book ***Event Modeling and Event Sourcing*** (Martin Dilger; method invented by **Adam Dymitruk**). Local copy (markdown, per-chapter): `~/Downloads/eventmodeling-md/eventmodeling-and-eventsourcing/`.

These capture **canonical event modeling** as the book defines it — the process this software is meant to support and enhance. They are **reference**, not our design decisions. Where our tool's design (the other `notes/` files) **diverges** from the book, the divergence is flagged in the relevant design note and/or here.

Researched via 5 parallel agents (2026-06-05), one per topic: process, element types, GWT/GT, benefits, organizing large models. Chapter citations like "(ch 13)" throughout.

> Caveat the book itself gives: the method is taught **narratively** through one worked e-commerce example, not as a numbered spec. Higher-level grouping (chapters/models) is explicitly an **optional extension**, not core. Many claims are the author's experiential opinion, not empirical.
